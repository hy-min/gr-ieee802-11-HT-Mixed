## Interpretation

**Verdict: H_BOTH_BROKEN** — H52 channel estimation is the root cause of L-SIG viterbi failure.

Both magnitude AND phase of the channel estimate diverge dramatically from the software loopback baseline. The divergence is NOT a small bias — it is structurally broken:

### Loopback aggregate stats (clean channel, n=1)
```
mean|H|=8.875  std|H|=0.000   mean(argH)=0.000  std(argH)=1.865
```
- **|H| perfectly uniform** (8.875 = kFftNormalize = 64/√52 ≈ 8.874)
- **arg(H) spans 52 subcarriers** with the standard L-LTF frequency-domain phase pattern (std=1.865 rad across the 52 SCs)
- This is the **ground truth** for a clean software loopback

### USRP aggregate stats (over-the-air, n=25 frames)
```
mean|H|=9.712  std|H|=8.643   mean(argH)=-0.056  std(argH)=1.778
```
- **|H| wildly variable across subcarriers** (std=8.64 means some SCs are 0.5x and others 2x loopback; per-SC std across frames ranges 3.5–13.2)
- **arg(H) drift** — mean is close to loopback (good), but per-frame per-SC values scatter randomly (USRP |H|_std_per_sc avg ~7 means each SC's magnitude flickers frame-to-frame)

### What this means

A correctly-estimated H should:
1. Have a **smooth** frequency response (|H[k]| varies slowly across adjacent subcarriers)
2. Be **stable** across consecutive frames (same channel, same H)

The USRP data violates BOTH:
- |H[0]=4.0 vs |H[2]=16.2 vs |H[5]=20.4 — adjacent SCs swing 5x
- Per-SC std across 25 frames: avg ~7 (some SCs have std 13) — channel flickers 50% per frame

This is consistent with:
- L-LTF0 RX is corrupted at the FFT-output level (e.g., timing misalignment, gain instability, FFT window drift)
- OR the FFT scaling between L-LTF0 and L-SIG is inconsistent
- OR the CFO/SFO compensation is being applied to L-SIG RX but the L-LTF0 used for H estimation is uncompensated (domain mismatch)
